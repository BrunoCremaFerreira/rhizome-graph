Five of these decide the design.

- **`environment: "node"` forbids ever importing the audio code in a test.** There is no
  `AudioContext` in the test environment and this repository's doctrine adds no mock to create
  one (`CLAUDE.md`, on shiki: keeping the suite "mock-free, jsdom-free and fast" is the stated
  reason for a whole module boundary). So the split in decision 5 is not a preference; the line
  is drawn for us and the only choice is which side each decision lands on.
- **0.093 us per call means the pure model is free.** At a burst of 200 events per second it is
  19 us per second of CPU, three orders below the 774 us per frame `fileColor` already spends
  (`docs/features/done/2026-08-25-22-17-size-mode.md`). Nothing in this feature needs to be fast;
  everything in it needs to be *quiet*, which is a different problem.
- **1 961 voices out of 200 000 events is a 100:1 reduction, and it comes from four independent
  rules**, not from one. Seeds silent, reads silent, a 40 ms floor and an 8-voice budget. Decision
  8 is that all four are needed and none of them is the safety net for the others.
- **A 15-note table gives 6 agents about 5 distinct pitches.** Two of six sharing a pitch is the
  ordinary case, not the pathological one, and no table size fixes it: 24 notes buys 5.40 against
  15's 5.09, for an octave of extra range nobody asked for. **Timbre cannot identify an agent**,
  and decision 6 says so out loud rather than implying otherwise.
- **The legend is already 162 characters and `bottomRow.ts`'s constants were measured against
  exactly that string.** Decision 11.

## 1. Assessment: what the page already does with an event, and where a sound could hang

### The seams, and which are load-bearing

**`main.ts`'s event sink is the one place every event passes through on the browser side**
(`main.ts:280-291`): `sim.applyEvent`, `renderer.onEvent`, `eventHud.push`,
`attribution.observe`, then the search refresh. **Five consumers, each a one-line call into a
module that decides for itself whether the event is any of its business.** `eventHud.push` returns
false for a seed (`eventLog.ts:82`) and for a read (`:90`); `attribution.observe` ignores seeds
(`attribution.ts:14-16`). A sixth consumer is one more line, and the shape of that line is already
established: **the sink offers, the module decides.** **Load-bearing, and this feature is one call
here plus one module behind it.**

**`eventLog.ts` already applies both silence rules this feature needs, and its comments give the
reasons.** Seed at `:82`: the daemon replays the whole tree on connect and "accepting it opens the
list full of backdrop". Read at `:83-90`: "an agent reads roughly ten times more often than it
writes, so accepting `R` would push every real edit off the top within seconds". Both sentences
transfer to sound word for word, with "the list" replaced by "the speakers". **Load-bearing, and
decision 7 is nothing but this rule with a different consequence.**

**`MAX_BEAMS = 512` with a shortened read beam is the precedent for a fixed budget on a
burst-driven channel** (`renderer.ts:139-140, 157-163`). The comment is explicit: reads arrive in
bursts, the buffer is fixed, so "a long-lived read beam would crowd the write beams out of the
buffer". A voice budget is the same reasoning applied to a resource that is not a buffer but an
audio thread. **Load-bearing, and decision 8 copies the shape.**

**`hashColor` is pure, exported and already the source of an agent's identity colour**
(`colors.ts:69-77`), and `renderer.ts:1240` is `hashColor("actor:" + agent)`. The hash is FNV-1a
over the agent id; the colour is `hue = (hash >>> 0) % 360` fed to `hslToInt`. **One hash, and a
second projection of it is a pitch.** That is what makes "sound and colour agree" a structural
fact rather than a coincidence, and it is the only reason this feature is interesting at all.
**Load-bearing, and decision 6 turns on it -- but see the defect below.**

**`sizeKeys.ts` is the template for an unconditional binding** (`:26-45`): all fields required, a
modified press declined, a **repeating** press declined, one command. Its docstring earns first
position in the keydown chain by contesting nothing (`:5-11`), and `main.ts:362-378` is the branch
that implements it. **Load-bearing, and decision 10 copies it.**

**`highlight.ts` is the precedent for confining a platform-or-vendor API to one module and
asserting it over the parsed source.** `CLAUDE.md`: "No shiki outside `highlight.ts`, not even
`import type`. That is what keeps the suite mock-free, jsdom-free and fast." The same assertion
shape is used for `checkouts.py` ("starts no process"), `content_search.py` ("imports no `re`"),
`sizes.py` ("opens nothing") and `window.py` ("never sees a token"). **Load-bearing, and decision 5
is the sixth instance of it.**

**`main.ts:322-348` is the single `reset` handler.** Every stateful thing on the page is cleared
there. **Load-bearing -- and decision 9 is that sound is the first thing on this page that must
NOT be cleared there**, which is the one place this feature contradicts an established pattern and
therefore the one that needs its reason written down.

### The four things that are actually in the way

1. **Nothing on the page makes a sound, and nothing may without a user gesture.** An
   `AudioContext` constructed on page load starts in the `suspended` state under every current
   autoplay policy, and `resume()` from anywhere but a gesture handler is rejected. So the context
   cannot be created at boot and handed around; its lifecycle is bound to the toggle.
2. **The test environment cannot see any of it** (`web/vitest.config.ts:6`). Whatever ends up in
   the audio module is code no assertion in this repository will ever execute.
3. **`hashColor` returns a colour, not a hash.** `colors.ts:69-77` computes the FNV-1a and then
   immediately reduces it to `% 360` and runs it through `hslToInt`; the 32-bit value never leaves
   the function. A pitch derived from `hashColor(...) % TABLE.length` is derived from *the
   colour*, which is the hash modulo 360 modulo 15 -- a double reduction that quietly correlates
   pitch with hue in a way nobody intended. Decision 6 and R3.
4. **The legend is full** (`web/index.html:26-27`), and `CONTEXT_WIDTH_FRACTION` was measured
   against its current length (`bottomRow.ts:20-25`).

### Two defects this feature exposes rather than creates

- **The `actor:` prefix is a literal inside `renderer.ts`** (`:1240`) while `hashColor` is pure and
  exported. This plan wants the same key, and so do
  `docs/features/todo/2026-08-26-20-56-attention-rules.md` (R10) and
  `docs/features/todo/2026-08-26-20-56-session-stats-panel.md` (R8). **Three plans, one prefix,
  and none of them can see the others' spelling of it.** R3 below is the same step those two
  file, extended by one export; whichever plan lands first does it and the others reuse.
- **`CLAUDE.md`'s Status section is stale on the frontend count** -- it says 1287, the tree says
  1403. Recorded so the next reader does not treat that document as a measurement.

## 2. Decisions before step 1

Fourteen decisions. Numbers 1, 6 and 13 are the ones I would most want argued with, and 13 is a
recommendation against part of the brief.

**1. Off by default, and the default is not a setting.** No environment variable, no `Settings`
field, no persistence in `localStorage`. The page loads silent, always, and stays silent until
someone presses the key in *this* tab. A page that starts making noise because of something
decided in another session, or in another tab, is the single worst failure available to this
feature -- and the cost of persistence is exactly that failure. *The price:* the toggle must be
pressed once per page load, which is the correct annoyance.

**2. Nothing about this reaches the daemon.** No command kind, no frame, no field. The browser
already receives every event it needs (`main.ts:280-291`), and the decision "does this make a
sound" is a function of the event and of local state. This keeps the feature's entire security
story one sentence: it turns no string from the network into anything, adds no route to
`resolve_inside`, and cannot be reached from a frame. That is the property `sizes` is praised for
in `server.py:481-487`, obtained here by adding no surface at all.

**3. One click per write, nothing on reads.** `eventLog.ts:83-90`'s reason, sharpened by the medium:
an agent reads roughly ten times more than it writes, and a list can be scrolled back while a
sound cannot. A read channel would be nine tenths of the noise carrying one tenth of the meaning.
*The price, stated because it is real:* the read glow was added precisely because "the graph used
to stay dark through the half of an agent's work that explains the other half" (`CLAUDE.md`), and
this feature deliberately re-opens that gap in the audio channel. The graph still shows reads; only
the speakers do not. **R11 records the trigger for reconsidering.**

**4. `A`, `M` and `D` all sound, and they sound the SAME.** The brief says "a click per write". A
separate timbre per operation would fight the timbre per agent for the same one perceptual
dimension, and the graph already answers "what kind of change" in colour (`normalize.py:32-35`:
`A` green, `M` amber, `D` red). One dimension, one meaning: **the voice says who, and the click
says that something happened.** *The price:* a deletion sounds like a write, and a `rm -rf` of a
directory is a burst of identical clicks. Decision 8's rate limit is what keeps that survivable,
and it is not a substitute for a distinct sound; it merely stops it being a wall.

**5. The split: `sound.ts` decides, `audio.ts` plays, and `AudioContext` is named in exactly one
module.** Forced by `web/vitest.config.ts:6` and modelled on `highlight.ts`.

*Pure, in `sound.ts`, and therefore testable:* whether an event makes a sound at all (seed, read,
toggle state); which voice it gets (the pitch, from the agent hash); what gain it gets; how long it
lasts; whether the rate limit and the voice budget allow it right now; and what the toggle's
lifecycle should be given `(enabled, documentHidden)`. **Everything that is a decision.**

*Impure, in `audio.ts`, and therefore never executed by a test:* constructing the `AudioContext`,
`resume()`/`suspend()`, creating an `OscillatorNode` and a `GainNode`, scheduling the envelope,
disconnecting on `ended`. **Everything that is an API call.** The module takes a fully-resolved
`Voice` value and does exactly what it says; it makes no decision of its own, not even "is this too
soon", because that is the rate limit and the rate limit is a decision.

*What this costs, said plainly:* **nothing about the actual sound is tested.** Not that a click is
audible, not that the envelope has no click-artefact at its edges, not that eight simultaneous
voices do not clip, not that `resume()` succeeds, not that a voice is disconnected rather than
leaked. The suite can prove that the model asks for the right thing and can never prove that the
right thing was done. *What it forbids:* adding jsdom, adding a mock `AudioContext`, or importing
`audio.ts` from any test -- all three would buy assertions about a fake and pay for them with the
property `CLAUDE.md` names as the reason the suite is fast and honest. **The whole of this
feature's verification is therefore a human with headphones**, and section 5 says so.

**6. The pitch and the colour are two projections of ONE hash, and the hash must be exposed as a
hash.** Today `hashColor` (`colors.ts:69-77`) computes the FNV-1a and reduces it in the same
breath. Deriving a pitch from `hashColor(...)` means deriving it from the *colour* -- hash mod 360
mod 15 -- which correlates pitch with hue by an arithmetic accident and makes two agents of nearby
hue disproportionately likely to share a pitch. So `colors.ts` grows `actorHash(agent): number`
returning the raw 32-bit value, and both `actorColor` and `actorVoice` are built on it. **One hash,
two projections**; the agreement between sound and colour is then a fact about the code rather
than a claim in a docstring.

*And the honest half:* **a timbre cannot identify an agent.** The measured table in section 0 says
six agents get about five distinct pitches from a 15-note table, and no table size fixes it -- 24
notes buys 0.31 of an agent for an octave of range. So the feature's claim is "sound and colour
agree", which is true and useful (the same agent always sounds the same, and its pitch is stable
across a reconnect), and **not** "you can tell which agent is working by ear", which is false. Any
UI text that implies the second is a lie.

**7. Seed events are silent, and the rule is stated locally rather than inherited.** A seed is up
to 12 524 events on this host's home directory (measured), arriving as fast as the socket delivers
them. `eventLog.ts:82` drops them and `attribution.ts:14-16` ignores them; this module does the
same, in its own line, with its own test. **It is not enough to rely on the rate limit** -- a
40 ms floor would still turn a 12 524-event seed into a 25-per-second machine-gun for the eight
minutes it takes to drain, which is precisely the "instant denial of service on the audio thread"
this decision exists to prevent, merely stretched out. The seed check comes **first**, before the
rate limit, so the limiter's own clock is never touched by backdrop.

**8. Four independent limits, and none is the safety net for the others.** Measured together they
take 200 000 events to 1 961 voices.
  - **Seed silent** (decision 7) -- removes the connect-time flood entirely.
  - **Reads silent** (decision 3) -- removes roughly nine tenths of live traffic.
  - **A rate floor**, `MIN_VOICE_INTERVAL_MS`. **Proposed 40 ms**, a ceiling of 25 voices per
    second, measured in section 0 alongside 30/60/80 ms. Derived from two things: 25/s is about
    where discrete clicks stop being countable and start being texture, and 40 ms is comfortably
    above the ~20 ms at which two clicks fuse into one perceived event. **Both of those are
    judgements from the literature and not measurements taken here**, and the constant is the
    first thing a human with headphones should retune.
  - **A voice budget**, `MAX_CONCURRENT_VOICES`. **Proposed 8**, chosen as `MAX_BEAMS / 64` in
    spirit rather than in arithmetic: it is the number above which simultaneous sine voices sum
    toward clipping at any per-voice gain worth hearing. Also unmeasured here.

  **When a limit is hit the event is DROPPED, never queued.** A queue turns a burst into a drone
  that outlives the work it describes -- a lie about "right now", which is the same objection
  `_broadcast_transient` makes to putting reads into the replay buffer (`server.py:394-396`: "a
  reconnecting client would replay stale read flashes -- a lie about 'right now'"). The sound must
  be either simultaneous with the event or absent.

**9. A root switch does NOT silence the audio, and this is the one place the feature contradicts
an established pattern.** `main.ts:322-348` clears everything on `reset`: the simulation, the
scene, the event list, the status panel, the open file, both searches, the size mode, the
attribution latch, the root bar. Every one of those holds something *about the old project*. The
audio toggle holds nothing about any project -- it is a property of the listener, not of the tree
-- and silencing it on `ctrl+L` would mean the user has to re-enable sound every time they change
what they are watching, which is the same annoyance as decision 1's per-page-load press repeated
without a reason. **What the reset must do is clear the rate limiter's clock and drop any pending
voice**, because the first event of the new project should not be suppressed by a floor set by the
last event of the old one. So: the toggle survives, the limiter does not. Say both halves in the
docstring, because "reset clears everything" is what the next reader will assume.

**10. The binding is F9, and it sits beside F7 above the keydown chain.** Unmodified, unclaimed by
browsers on a page, contested by nothing, and conditional on nothing -- sound has to toggle with
the file viewer open, with the root bar focused and with either search bar taking keystrokes, for
`sizeKeys.ts:5-11`'s stated reason: "the chain below is ordered by CONTESTED keys, and a binding
that contests nothing takes no part in that argument." `interpretSoundKey` declines every modified
press and every **repeating** press, the latter with a sharper reason than F7's: held down, F9
repeats at roughly 30 Hz, and every second repeat would construct or tear down an `AudioContext`
-- a real platform resource, not a state field. `preventDefault`, for the same reason F7 does it.
*If `2026-08-26-20-56-session-stats-panel.md` also ships*, F7/F8/F9 are three unconditional
bindings stacked above the chain; that is fine while each declines everything but its own key, and
R4 step 4.4 is the enumeration that keeps it fine.

**11. The legend gains 12 characters, and that is a browser measurement nobody here can take.**
`" - F9: sound"` takes the legend from 162 to 174 characters, ~988 px to ~1061 px at
`MAX_GLYPH_PX = 6.1`. `CONTEXT_WIDTH_FRACTION = 0.34` is not arithmetic; `bottomRow.ts:20-25` says
it was measured, that "0.34 keeps the shortcut legend at two lines at both 1280 and 1600, where
0.40 wraps it to three at 1280", and that "the legend is the widest thing in the row, so it is what
pays for a greedy centre". A 7% growth moves where it wraps and the constant has no idea.
**What it displaces, if it must displace something:** `F3: next` is the weakest entry -- it is
meaningless unless a search is already open, and both search bars show their own counter when they
are. Second weakest is `double-click: auto-fit`, which users find by accident. **Do not decide
that here.** The step is: add the entry, then re-measure at 1280 and 1600, and only then choose
between shortening an entry and lowering the fraction. If the stats panel ships too, both entries
land together (194 characters, ~1183 px) and the measurement is taken once for both.

**12. The toggle IS the gesture, and the context is built inside the key handler.** Lifecycle,
in full:
  - **Page load:** no `AudioContext` exists. Nothing is constructed, nothing is `suspended`, and
    the entry chunk grows by the size of two small modules and nothing else -- WebAudio is a
    platform API, so there is no dependency, no chunk and no lazy import to arrange.
  - **First F9:** construct the context *inside the keydown handler*, so the construction is
    inside a user gesture; call `resume()` in the same turn. This is the only moment a gesture is
    guaranteed, which is why the context cannot be built at boot and enabled later.
  - **F9 again:** `suspend()`, not `close()`. A closed context cannot be reopened and the next F9
    would have to build another; suspending costs a handful of kilobytes of idle graph and makes
    the toggle instant.
  - **Tab backgrounded:** `document.visibilityState === "hidden"` -> `suspend()`. Browsers already
    throttle or suspend audio in background tabs, inconsistently; doing it explicitly makes the
    behaviour ours rather than the vendor's. **A window behind another window is not hidden** --
    `visibilitychange` fires for a backgrounded *tab*, not an occluded one -- so a user who
    switches applications keeps hearing the session, which is arguably the whole point of the
    feature. Say that, because "it went quiet when I alt-tabbed" and "it kept playing when I
    alt-tabbed" are both plausible expectations and only one of them is what happens.
  - **Tab foregrounded:** `resume()` **only if the toggle is still on**. The decision is
    `shouldRun(enabled, hidden)`, two lines, pure, tested. The API calls that follow from it are
    not.
  - **Root switch:** the context is untouched (decision 9).
  - **Page unload:** nothing. The context dies with the document.

**13. Ship ONE voice first. Per-agent timbre is a second step, and it should not be built until
somebody has listened to the first.** This is where I am not ratifying the brief. The brief asks
for a timbre per agent, and decision 6 shows how to do it correctly -- but it also shows what it
buys: six agents get five pitches, so the per-agent part adds *variety*, not *identification*. The
risk this feature actually carries is not "the pitches are wrong", it is "any sound at all is
maddening within ten minutes", and that risk is fully exposed by a single click at a fixed pitch.
**A one-voice version is R1+R2+R4+R5 and can be listened to in an afternoon; the per-agent hash is
R3, is genuinely nice, and is worthless if the answer to the afternoon is "turn it off".** Build in
that order. *The price of the order:* R3 is a small step that gets deferred and might get forgotten,
which is a bookkeeping cost against a real chance of not writing R3 at all.

**14. No dependency, and there is nothing to justify.** `CLAUDE.md` requires a new runtime
dependency to name what it replaces and what it costs in the entry chunk; this feature adds none.
The two named alternatives are both refusals: a synthesis library (Tone.js and friends) would be a
third runtime dependency, larger than shiki, for four oscillators and an envelope; and a bundled
audio *sample* would be bytes in `dist/` for a sound that a 90 ms sine with an exponential decay
already makes. The entry chunk stays 551 195 bytes plus two small modules, and R5 step 5.4 is the
measurement that keeps it honest.

## 3. The plan, and the ranking that comes before it

**Overall rank: `next`, with R3 and R6 as `noted`.** Ranked below the other two plans of
2026-08-26 and below anything with a user waiting for it.

**Why `next` and not `now`.** The other two features answer questions a user has asked -- "did an
agent touch my workflow file", "what did this session do". This one answers a question nobody has
asked, in a channel nobody can evaluate from a description. Its whole value is a judgement --
*is a click per write informative, or is it a dripping tap?* -- and there is exactly one way to
find out.

**What would settle it, and why nobody here can do it.** Someone sits with headphones through a
real agent session of twenty minutes or more, with the toggle on, doing something else, and reports
whether they noticed the session's rhythm or reached for the key. **This host is a tty**: no
`DISPLAY`, no browser, no audio device. No agent working in this repository can make that judgement
or any part of it, and no test can stand in for it. The afternoon of listening is not a
verification step appended to the work -- it is the step that decides whether the work was worth
doing, and decision 13 puts it as early in the sequence as it can go.

**If the answer is "turn it off":** delete `sound.ts`, `audio.ts`, `soundKeys.ts`, one branch in
`main.ts` and one legend entry. Nothing else in the tree will have changed, which is itself a
reason to build it in this shape.

Ranked, ordered, every step one RED test plus one GREEN implementation, both suites green between
any two steps. R1 and R4 depend on nothing and can land first, in either order.

New test files throughout, so no existing assertion moves: `web/tests/sound.test.ts`,
`web/tests/soundKeys.test.ts`, `web/tests/soundVoice.test.ts`.

### R1 -- Nothing decides whether an event is worth hearing. **Rank: now (within the feature), and it can land first**

**What is missing.** `main.ts:280-291` offers each event to five consumers and none of them is
about sound. No module holds a rate limiter, a voice budget or a toggle.

**Where.** New pure module `web/src/sound.ts`. Beside `search.ts`, `contentSearch.ts`,
`fileView.ts`, `sizeMode.ts` and `eventLog.ts`, for the reason all five give: a decision taken in
`main.ts` carries no test by doctrine, and one taken in a module that touches a platform API cannot
be tested at all. **Not** in `eventLog.ts`: that module is the recent-changes list and its drop
rules are that list's, not a shared policy -- two callers of one `shouldDrop` would be one change
away from silencing the list to quieten the speakers. **Not** in `simulation.ts`: sound has a
lifetime of its own and `SimNode` is four decaying channels, the same objection `sizeMode.ts:19-22`
makes to putting `bytes` there.

**Why it costs to put it elsewhere.** The predictable next change is a fifth limit -- a per-agent
floor so one busy agent cannot monopolise the channel, or a gain that falls with the event rate.
In its own module that is one field and one test. Inside `main.ts` it is untestable by doctrine,
and inside `eventLog.ts` it is a change to the module that paints the panel a user is reading.

**Target shape.**

```
MIN_VOICE_INTERVAL_MS = 40      // 25 voices/s ceiling; measured table in section 0
MAX_CONCURRENT_VOICES = 8

interface Voice {
  readonly freq: number;        // Hz
  readonly gain: number;        // 0..1
  readonly durationMs: number;
}

interface SoundState {
  readonly enabled: boolean;
  readonly lastVoiceMs: number;
  readonly live: number;
}

createSound(): SoundState
toggleSound(state): SoundState
voiceFor(state, event, nowMs): Voice | null   // null means silence
noteStarted(state, nowMs): SoundState
noteEnded(state): SoundState
resetLimiter(state): SoundState               // decision 9: the clock, not the toggle
shouldRun(enabled, hidden): boolean           // decision 12
```

Six properties hold it up, and each is a test.

- **Disabled is silent, first and unconditionally.** Before the seed check, before everything.
- **A seed event is silent, and the check comes BEFORE the rate limiter** so a 12 524-event
  backdrop never touches `lastVoiceMs`. Decision 7, and it is the test that stops someone
  "simplifying" the order.
- **A read is silent.** Decision 3.
- **The floor drops, it does not queue.** Two events 10 ms apart yield one voice and one `null`;
  the second is gone, not deferred.
- **The budget drops too**, and `live` is maintained by the caller through `noteStarted` /
  `noteEnded` rather than by a timer inside the model -- a timer is a decision the model cannot
  test without owning a clock, and the real end of a note is an event the platform reports.
- **`voiceFor` is a pure function of its three arguments.** No module-level state, no `Date.now`,
  no `performance`. The clock arrives as a parameter, the way the viewport arrives as a parameter
  in `labels.ts` and `bottomRow.ts`.

**Worst case, in the units that matter.** 0.093 us per call, measured over 200 000 events on this
host. At the highest burst this page has seen -- a seed of 12 524 events -- that is 1.2 ms total,
once, and every one of those calls returns `null` at the seed check.

### R3 -- The agent hash is not available as a hash. **Rank: noted within this feature; `next` as a shared step**

**What is wrong.** `colors.ts:69-77` computes FNV-1a and immediately reduces it: `const hue =
(hash >>> 0) % 360; return hslToInt(hue, 0.7, 0.6);`. The 32-bit value never escapes. And
`renderer.ts:1240` spells the key as `hashColor("actor:" + agent)`, so the `"actor:"` prefix is a
literal inside a module no test can execute.

**Why it costs.** Two costs, and the second is the one this plan cares about. **(a)** Three plans
of 2026-08-26 want an agent's identity-derived value -- the alarm swatch in
`2026-08-26-20-56-attention-rules.md` R10, the stats row in
`2026-08-26-20-56-session-stats-panel.md` R8, and this one -- and each would respell the prefix.
**(b)** A pitch taken from `hashColor(...) % 15` is a pitch taken from the *colour*: hash mod 360
mod 15, a double reduction that correlates pitch with hue and quietly shrinks the effective table.
The feature's one claim -- "sound and colour agree" -- deserves to be true because both are
projections of one hash, not true because one is computed from the other.

**Target shape.** In `colors.ts`:

```

```

`PITCH_TABLE` is the `PREFERENCE_BY_PLATFORM` idiom (`CLAUDE.md`: "the one table a spike result
may edit; nothing else changes"): a listening session may rewrite the notes and must not have to
touch anything else. Proposed content: a pentatonic set over three octaves from A3, 15 entries --
pentatonic because any two members are consonant, so two agents working at once do not produce an
interval that reads as a mistake. **This is a musical judgement, not a measurement**, and section
5 says so.

**What must NOT be claimed.** Section 0's table: six agents get about 5.09 distinct pitches from
15 notes, ten agents get 7.48. Two agents sharing a pitch is ordinary. The docstring must say
"the same agent always sounds the same" and must not say "you can tell agents apart by ear".

  a uniform shape, with a `voiceFor` I wrote for the measurement rather than the one R1 specifies.
  It establishes the order of magnitude and nothing finer.
- I did not investigate whether any browser this project targets refuses `OscillatorNode` under a
  restrictive permissions policy, nor whether a WebKitGTK webview (`window.py`'s default backend on
  Linux) has a working audio path at all. **That last one is a real gap**: `rhi` opens a webview by
  default, so the default way to run this application may be the one way this feature cannot work,
  and nobody here can check it. It belongs in the same spike as the WebGL questions
  `tools/webview_spike.py` already carries.

## Consultation: `security-auditor` (2026-08-26)

Appended by the orchestrator. The audit covered all five plans of this batch **together** and
ranked one critical, five high and seven medium findings; the full report is
`docs/security/2026-08-26-audit-five-planned-features.md` and it is the authority. This section is a pointer into it, never a second
copy of it. It was written against the feature descriptions, **not** against this document --
the auditor states so itself -- so where the two disagree the disagreement is real and unresolved,
not an editing slip.

### Findings that land on this plan

- **M4 (medium).** A connecting client is replayed the whole seed -- up to `DEFAULT_MAX_FILES = 20 000` `A`
  events inside a second. A click per write that does not drop seed fires 20 000 times.
  `eventLog.push` already drops seed before the fold and the sound sink must inherit that rule,
  and drop `R` besides. `AudioContext` starts `suspended` under every current autoplay policy, so
  off-by-default is not only right but the only workable shape. 8 concurrent voices, 20 clicks per
  second, from the same 60 Hz budget.
- **M3 (medium).** A voice per agent on a map that never evicts. Browsers cap concurrent audio nodes far lower
  than textures, and an exhausted `AudioContext` is not recoverable without a reload. Allocate
  voices from a pool of 32, on the model of the 24 read-marker sprites.
- **M1 (medium).** Applies only if this feature ever adds a frame; as planned it adds none.

### The auditor's own summary of this feature

**5. Ambient sound -- the non-event, with two sharp edges.** No new input crosses any boundary;
everything it consumes is already parsed and validated. The edges are the 20 000-event seed
replay (M4) and the per-agent voice on an unbounded actor map (M3). Both are ordinary capacity
bugs with numbers attached, and neither is a security property.
